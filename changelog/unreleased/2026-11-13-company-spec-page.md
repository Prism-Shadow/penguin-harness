# The landing site gets a draft spec for the next company mode at /company, with a prototype of both setting up and day to day

- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Type:** feature
- **Scope:** `landing`, `ui-gallery`

[中文版](2026-11-13-company-spec-page.zh.md)

penguin.ooo/company is a draft spec for the next company mode, written to the light, self-running organization the company-mode research settled on: you agree goals with one lead agent, an executor and an independent verifier move each ticket along a state machine, work that fails verification goes back on its own, and only blockers, breakers and final calls reach your inbox. Each domain is reviewed in a workbench of its own, and every call and send-back is written to local files the organization learns from. The page's main exhibit is a prototype of the mode, built of the Web App's own shell and controls on sample data and framed live on the page, in which both setting up and day-to-day handling can be tried. The shipped company mode is unchanged, and the page links to its docs. The page has no entry in the navigation or the footer; it is reached by its address.

## Details

- **Setting up.** The prototype opens on a company that has not started. The lead lists the machines, models and outside CLIs it found, then asks what you want done: pick a starter card (one-person company, software maintenance, research, vertical operations), write a goal in a sentence, or import an organization template pack. It then asks three things on an A2UI form you click through: the kinds of work, the budget per ticket and the verifier's send-back limit, plus whether outside events file tickets directly. From the answers it drafts a plan you can change before confirming: the work, the team (1 lead, 1 to 3 executors and 1 verifier by default, with each executor's model swappable and every member's machine and permissions shown), the state machine, the two limits, the approval grade of each outward action, the triggers, the workbench for each kind of work, the first tickets and two warnings. Confirming lands you in the inbox with the first tickets assigned. While setting up, the left column shows the four steps.
- **Day to day.** The prototype can also open on Floe Studio, a one-person company a week in, at the ticket that needs you most. The inbox is grouped into your call, in progress and settled, and a row with an unsent draft is highlighted. Each ticket shows a status bar (working, verifying, your call, done, each with its holder), the budget and the send-backs used, and below them what it waits on you for: a blocker is an A2UI decision card whose answer is written back to the ticket and optionally to the handbook; a breaker says whether the budget or the send-back limit was hit, and offers more budget or another round, your own call, or a stop; verified work is accepted by the grade of its outward action (one-click approval, approval after review, or a prefilled draft you send yourself), or sent back with a note; a proposal from the lead is accepted or declined. A ticket has three tabs, the record, the workbench and the runs: the workbench is a diff and its checks, experiment curves and a metrics table, a long document with versions and per-paragraph comments, a contract ledger and invoice reconciliation, or prefilled replies; the runs list each run's model, tokens, cache hits and cost, with the execution log folded. Let time pass in the bottom bar moves every ticket one step: deliveries go to the verifier, failed verification sends work back, tickets at a limit halt, and GitHub issues and outside messages file tickets. A send-back becomes a lesson and a new version of the member's Skill.
- **The other pages.** The lead is the one way in for new work: say a sentence, the lead answers with a draft ticket (executor, verifier, workbench, limits, outward action) and files it once you confirm. The Team page lists each member's role, machine, model (swappable), permissions, first-pass rate, rework and cost per ticket, imports and exports template packs, and notes outside CLIs as an extension. The Rules page holds the state machine, the budget and send-back limits, the outward grades, the trigger switches and three things the mode will not do. Handbook & skills lists the decisions your calls recorded, the lessons send-backs recorded, each member's Skill versions (with rollback) and the organization's files. Review & benchmarks lists first-pass rate, rework and cost per member; Review now has the lead propose an improvement ticket, and the Shared RSI Community's template scores sit below. A checklist drawer lists 4 things to try in setting up and 11 day to day, ticks what you have done, and restarts either part.
- **The prototype's rules.** Its state (`model.ts`) keeps the spec's gates: only a ticket's holder in its current status moves it; unverified work never reaches the inbox; the server halts a ticket at its send-back limit or its budget; outward actions wait for your approval; an answer is written to the ticket before it goes back to the session; a Skill or model is snapshotted before it changes. `test/company-model.test.ts` checks it against them.
- **The page.** It opens on what the page is, then frames the prototype in the site's language and mode; the window bar picks setting up or day to day, and below it the two parts' things to try sit side by side, each with a button that starts there. Six sections follow: the architecture (the delivery line, the automatic send-back inside the state machine, the two-loop learning) with the first version's four problems and this version's answers; what is borrowed from T3 Code, Lody, Raft, Multica and Grok Bot, how it lands, and where the prototype shows it; where it stops (the delivery organization apart from a mirror of a real company, four things it will not do, outside CLIs as an extension); the two problems no product solved (general tickets against expert review, and getting steadier as it runs), with the five workbenches and the three measures a review reads; thirteen hard gates on the server, each with the answer a refused write gets and whether the first version has it; and the ticket file field by field, with the four rules that read waiting on you from the fields.
- **Each fact in one place.** The statuses, holders, transitions, waiting rule, inbox groups, outward actions and grades, workbenches, triggers, fields and gates live in the gallery's `src/company/spec.ts`; the prototype acts on it and the page tables it.
- **A shared window.** The landing's live app window is split in two: `LiveFrame` frames any gallery document (loaded on demand, scaled to its column, the page's guest), and `LiveAppFrame` frames the app through it. The home page's window is unchanged.
- **The site.** The page is its own chunk, loaded on demand. Its tab title and description follow the reader's language; the static shell carries the page's own title and description, and the share card is the site's. `/company/` is in the sitemap.
