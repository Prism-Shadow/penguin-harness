# The landing site gets a draft spec for the next company mode at /company, with a prototype to click through

- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Type:** feature
- **Scope:** `landing`, `ui-gallery`

[中文版](2026-11-13-company-spec-page.zh.md)

penguin.ooo/company is a draft spec for the next company mode. Its main exhibit is a prototype of the mode, built of the Web App's own shell and controls on sample data, framed live on the page: a person tells one lead agent what is new, it files the work as tickets, and each ticket is done by a work session of its own; the ticket is the record, the session is the agent's conversation while it works. A ticket reaches the person only to be accepted, answered or reviewed, and the person can also open a session and talk to it directly. Under the prototype the page lists what changes against the shipped mode, how the company gets better, what the server enforces, and the ticket file field by field. The shipped company mode is unchanged, and the page links to its docs. The page has no entry in the navigation or the footer; it is reached by its address.

## Details

- **Prototype.** A fourth gallery document, `company.html` (`packages/ui-gallery/src/company`), mounted under the app's own providers like the library boards. The window has two columns. The left is the inbox, and it is the whole navigation: a line to the lead at its head (⌘K or Ctrl+K from anywhere), then what waits on the reader, what is in progress and the schedules, each ticket row showing its title and what it asks, delivered or last did; the reader's own piles (later, handled, archived) are one line at its foot. The right column reads what the left names, and opens on the first ticket that waits on the reader. The team, the handbook and the board sit on a bar above it, beside what the company has spent this month; the calendar and finance lose their pages, the spend moving to that bar, the team page and each ticket. A ticket's record and its work sessions are tabs of one view; where the shipped dialog shows a blocked reason, a ticket that waits on the reader takes the answer at the top of its record: accept or reject a proposal, pick an option of its one question, accept a delivery or send it back with a note. A team page takes the org chart's place: one row per employee with its name, title, who it reports to, its duties, what it has spent of its monthly budget and the work sessions it has open; a role the lead proposed and the reader has not accepted is listed as proposed. Every byline on a ticket, a session and the board shows the employee's title. The handbook is sorted by purpose into the index, decisions, lessons, skills and documents: an answer is filed as a decision, a send-back as a lesson, an accepted document delivery as a document, and an accepted skill ticket installs its skill for its employee; every file links the ticket it came from, and a ticket's record lists the files it added. A budget crossing its warning line becomes a ticket for the reader to answer; the answer sets the budget, and carrying it out closes the ticket with nothing to review. The company gets better in two loops. Every ticket: an answer is filed as a decision and a send-back as a lesson, and the employee whose work was sent back or corrected notes it in its own `notes` skill with no approval, the team page showing each employee's first-pass reviews, send-backs, cost per finished ticket and what it learned. Every N closed tickets (10 in the spec, 5 in the sample company, with the progress under the inbox's schedules), the lead runs a retrospective, filed in the handbook, that compares the numbers with the last one and says what followed the changes accepted last time, and files the changes it wants as proposals; an accepted skill takes the lesson it came from out of the handbook's index. The lead's conversation files what the reader says as a ticket. In a work session the reader talks to the working agent directly; while the session waits on them, the same answer or review block the ticket shows sits above its input. A company with nothing yet asks one sentence, and the lead answers with proposed tickets. No agent runs: a bar under the page moves every work session one step when the reader asks.
- **Rules in the prototype.** Its state (`model.ts`) keeps the spec's rules: an action is taken only on a ticket that waits for it; a ticket turns unread only when a step leaves it waiting, which also brings it back from "later"; a work session stops on its question until it is answered, and the answer is written to the ticket, then goes into the same session as the person's message, with no second session opened; a note sending a delivery back goes into the session that delivered it; what the person says in a session is written to the ticket too; a schedule's ticket gets no session before it is accepted, unless that schedule was switched to start work directly; a ticket that waits cannot be set aside or archived. `test/company-model.test.ts` holds it to them.
- **The page.** The opening says what the page is, then frames the prototype in the site's language and mode; the window bar chooses the state it opens in (a company a week in, or one before its first sentence) and links to the prototype on its own. Then four sections: what changes against the shipped mode, as rows of the matter, today and the spec; how the company gets better, with the two loops, the four curves a retrospective reads (first-pass reviews and send-backs, questions, repeated mistakes, cost per ticket) and how the handbook is kept short; the rules the server enforces, numbered, each with the answer a refused write gets and whether it is enforced today; and the ticket file field by field, with the three frontmatter fields (`source`, `ask`, `deliverables`), the `## Decisions` section and the three per-person triage values (`unread`, `snoozed_until`, `archived`) the spec adds. The rules gain three: an employee's own skill is never overwritten by a plugin update, a retrospective comes due by the count of closed tickets, and a change is snapshotted before it is made. "Waiting on a person" is read from the five shipped statuses: `proposed`, `review`, or `in_progress` with a person in `blocked_by`.
- **One place per kind of content.** The statuses, the waiting rule, the inbox views, the fields and the rules are in the gallery's `src/company/spec.ts`, which the prototype acts on and the page tables.
- **Shared frame.** The landing's live app frame is split: `LiveFrame` frames any gallery document (lazy, scaled to the column, a guest of the page) and `LiveAppFrame` keeps framing an app surface through it. The home page's frames are unchanged.
- **Site.** The page is its own chunk, loaded on demand. Its tab title and description follow the reader's language; its static shell carries its own title and description with the site's share card. `/company/` is in the sitemap.
