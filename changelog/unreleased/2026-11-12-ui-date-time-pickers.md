# Date and time pickers in the shared UI package

- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Type:** feature
- **Scope:** `ui`, `ui-gallery`, `web`
- **PR:** [#1547](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/penguin-harness/pull/1547)

[中文版](2026-11-12-ui-date-time-pickers.zh.md)

The shared UI package (`@prismshadow/penguin-ui`) gained four picker fields — `DateField`, `TimeField`, `DateTimeField` and `DateRangeField` — whose calendar follows Material 3's date pickers and whose times turn on iOS-style wheels. They write the native inputs' formats (`YYYY-MM-DD`, `HH:mm`, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm`, local and without a zone), so a caller swaps a native input for one without changing what it stores. The gallery's inputs board shows them in place of the native inputs.

## Details

- **The field** sits on the shared control look, the same height and focus ring as an Input or a Select of its rung, and shows the value the way the reader's locale writes it: 2026年11月12日 / Nov 12, 2026, 14:30 / 2:30 PM, a span as its two dates in full, 2026年11月1日-2026年11月16日 / Nov 1, 2026 – Nov 16, 2026. An empty field shows a placeholder; an optional field holding a value shows a clear cross in place of its calendar or clock mark; a required, refused or closed field reads as the other fields do. With a `name` the value is also posted in a hidden input.
- **The calendar** is Material 3's docked picker: a popover under the field (above it when there is no room), portaled over dialogs and transcripts. Under 640px it is Material 3's modal form, a full-width sheet along the bottom edge over a scrim, headed by the field's title. It shows a month with its weekdays as narrow names in the locale's order (一 二 … Monday first for zh-CN, S M … Sunday first for en-US), the full name on hover; six weeks, so it never changes height; days in the theme's control shape (a disc in Frost, a square in Console), the picked day filled with the accent, today in the accent's ink inside a hairline ring, days outside `min` / `max` faded and not pickable; a year and a month menu button in the locale's order, the ‹ › month steps and Today. A menu button swaps the grid for three columns of months or years, the current one filled; a press brings the grid back on it, and the button pressed again leaves the month as it was. A new month arrives under the theme's reveal. The popover keeps each theme's own surface, radius, border, shadow, type size, hover and focus ring rather than Material 3's tonal container, large radius, ripple and large headline.
- **The wheels** for hours, minutes and, on a 12-hour clock, the day period move one row a mouse-wheel notch (a trackpad one row per row's height), take a press on a row or a drag, and scroll natively with snapping under a finger, without scrolling the page behind; a minute step (1, 5, 15) sets the minute rows. Typed digits jump to a value and move on to the next wheel once nothing longer can be typed, so "1430" sets 14:30; Enter or Done closes the panel. The date-and-time field puts a time row and Done under its calendar, whose pill swaps in the wheels.
- **The range field** heads its calendar with the span as it stands, an end not picked yet named Start date or End date. Two presses in either order set the ends, the days between them shaded on a flat band, and a third press starts over; OK writes the span and closes the panel, while Cancel, Escape and a press outside write nothing. It opens on the month of the span's end, and carries no presets: a caller's own control holds those.
- **Keyboard and assistive technology**: the field is a button named by its title and value that opens a labelled dialog; the grid follows the ARIA date-grid pattern (arrows, Home / End, Page Up / Page Down, with Shift a year, Enter); the month and year menus are listboxes (arrows, Home / End, Enter or Space); each time wheel is a spin button (↑ / ↓, Page Up / Page Down, Home / End, typed digits or letters, Enter); the clear cross's press target is at least 24px; Escape closes the popover and leaves a dialog around the field open, and the focus returns to the field.
- Dates are reckoned on the calendar day itself, never through the reader's zone, so no day shifts west of Greenwich or across a daylight-saving change. Words and the locale come from a new `dates` group of `UiStrings`, which the Web App fills from both dictionaries.
