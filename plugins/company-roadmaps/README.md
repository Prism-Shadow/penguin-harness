# Company roadmaps

Roadmaps for company mode: a person — or an employee, asked to by one — puts **one or more employees in a room** — a channel the roadmap opens for itself and the channel list leaves out — to discuss something too big for one proposal. The room is **an ordinary channel**: each employee takes part from **its own desk**, which the organization wakes for the room's messages as for any channel's. The first employee named moderates and keeps the **draft**: a record of the discussion, a **body** written as a paper, and the **items** it leads to — proposals (a brief and an owner, stacked on one another) and roadmaps (a brief and the employees who discuss them), each citing the body sections it draws on. Nothing is created while the room discusses. **Establishing** the roadmap ends the discussion: a roadmap item derives its roadmap at once, but a proposal item stays **a brief** — nothing is created for it and its owner is not told — until it has its **approvals** — by default the moderator's and one other member's (a person or an employee). An owner who finds, while building, that the roadmap lacks something it needs **reopens** the room.

It depends on [company-proposals](../company-proposals/README.md), and must be installed with it: an approved item's proposal is created there, through that plugin's module (`createFromRoadmap`), and linked back. Every write of this plugin is a roadmap Action run through company-proposals' Action registry (`POST …/actions/<key>/runs`); this plugin's routes are the reads.

## What you get

- **A room that is a channel nobody else sees.** Opening a roadmap opens its room: an organization channel (`roadmap_<n>`) with the employees picked and the person who opened it (an employee who opens it is recorded as its creator, and is in it only when it names itself among the employees), made through the organization gateway (`OrgGateway.openRoom`) and **unlisted** — the channel list, in the sidebar and in `penguin org channel ls`, leaves it out, so the way in is the sidebar's ROADMAPS section and the roadmaps page. The room is read and spoken in on the app's own channel page — the same stream, composer and phone layout as any channel — which, for a roadmap's room, shows the roadmap in a column beside the stream. Everything else is the channel's own: posting, inviting and removing, archiving, and the delivery of its messages. The plugin writes nothing to it but its members, which follow the roadmap's (below), and reads only its configuration. (A roadmap may still be opened over an existing channel through the API, with `channelId`.) An organization that runs on another machine has its room opened there: this server refuses (409 `org_runs_elsewhere`), since it holds only a mirror that the next copy would overwrite.
- **The desk takes part.** The plugin opens no session of its own. At the opening — and when a room is bound to a roadmap that was waiting for one — each member's desk gets one line: the room, who moderates, how to speak there (`penguin org channel send`), and for the moderator how to keep the draft and establish the roadmap; in a room a person opened, the moderator speaks first, to that person, and the others hold back until one of the two speaks to them. From then on the room's messages reach the members' desks through the organization's own delivery (the Dev of the roadmap in its Claude Code session, when that is its desk), and an exchange between employees stops at the organization's mention-chain limit, as in any channel.
- **The members are the room's.** `roadmap.members` changes the roadmap's members and its room's channel together: an added employee joins the channel (and, while the room discusses, its desk is told it is there), a removed one leaves it. A reopening brings a member who left the channel meanwhile back in; other employees in the channel stay.
- **A draft, then an establishment.** The moderator (or anyone of the organization) writes the record, the body and the items; establishing checks every cite against the body's headings, marks the roadmap established, and turns every roadmap item into a derived roadmap with a room of its own, discussing at once (waiting for a room only when one could not be opened).
- **A proposal only after its approvals.** At the establishment a proposal item becomes a brief and nothing more: no proposal is created, its owner is not told, and no text anywhere tells anyone to create one. It needs the approvals of that brief in its approval roles (`moderator` and `member` by default — the moderator's, whose desk is asked for them at the establishment, and any other member's, a person's **Approve** beside the item in the roadmap's column or an employee's), each recorded with its role, who and when, and one per principal (the `roadmap.item.approve` Action on `item:<n>/<key>`). A company workflow that replaces that Action's guard sets other roles (`moderator`, `member`, `person`, `employee`, `any`) by handing them to the default it wraps: `(defaults) => (input, options) => defaults(input, { ...options, roles: ["moderator", "person"] })` (`withApprovalRoles` is the same, for code that can import this package). The last approval creates the proposal in company-proposals — its owner the author, the item's title and brief its own, the `created` line naming the roadmap and the item — and links it, in the same step; a creation that fails fails the approval, which is not recorded and can be given again. Its owner's desk then gets one line: the item, who approved it in which role and when, the proposal's number and what it is stacked on — and no command. An item that is a proposal which exists already has nothing to start, so it is not approved: anyone but its own owner links it to that proposal while it is still a brief (`roadmap.item.link`), and it is delegated at once with no approvals and no desk line. A brief that changes at a later establishment starts again with no approvals, and keeps its link: approved again while the linked proposal is open, it creates nothing — that proposal's brief is rewritten to the new one (its revisions, comments and approvals stand, its author is told) and the owner's line says so; only a merged or rejected proposal gets a new one, linked in its place. No desk line this plugin sends carries a write command.
- **An existing proposal taken in.** A proposal that already exists joins the roadmap as it is (`roadmap.adopt` on `roadmap:<n>` with `{ proposal, title, owner, brief? }`, while the room discusses or once established; the page's **Add an existing proposal** offers the organization's proposals not in it yet). It is a proposal item with its number (`proposal`, no cites needed): nothing is created, nothing waits for approvals, no desk is told; an establishment delegates it and links its number at once.
- **Stacking.** A proposal item is stacked on the previous proposal item unless it names another (`stackedOn`) or none (`stackedOn: null`). When the one below gets its proposal number — at its second approval, or by a link — the owners stacked on it are told.
- **ROADMAPS in the sidebar, and the roadmap beside its room.** Company mode's sidebar has a **ROADMAPS** section below the channel list, apart from "My channels": the five most recently active roadmaps under discussion, the rest behind "Show n more", a **+** that opens a roadmap, and **All roadmaps**. A row opens the roadmap's room — the app's channel page, with the roadmap in a column on the right, drawn by the app itself from `GET …/roadmaps/:n` and re-read every 10 s: the moderator's face and name, the items first (shaped like the proposals queue: a linked item is its proposal's row, a brief shows its approvals), then the body rendered as Markdown — `proposal:<n>` is a capsule, a footnote a note. The record is not shown there. On a narrow window the stream keeps the screen and a **Roadmap** bar swaps the column in. The org nav has no Roadmaps row. **All roadmaps** is `/org/<project>/<org>/roadmaps`, a page this plugin serves itself (in an iframe): the roadmaps one line each, as the proposals list does (number, name, status, room, moderator, how many items, when it last moved; the items are on the roadmap's own detail), and an **Open a roadmap** button whose dialog goes straight to the new roadmap's room. Asked about an organization that runs on another machine, the page asks that machine through `/server/<machine>/`, so the plugin must be installed there as well. The page wears the app's look rather than one of its own: it links the app's stylesheet for framed pages (`/workflow-ui.css`), copies the app's resolved theme from the window around it — dark or light, the gray scale, the accent, the font and the font size — and follows it when it changes. It says what it is waiting for, and why when it cannot read (the HTTP status, or no answer in 15 s).
- **Three states, and reopening.** A roadmap is `awaiting_room` (derived, no room yet), `discussing`, or `established`; nothing else. Anyone of the organization reopens an established roadmap with a reason (`roadmap.reopen`), which reaches every member's desk. A roadmap has a name (renamed at will). A roadmap is never archived: to stop a room's discussion, archive the channel itself.

## Boundaries

- **A room's messages wake desks as any channel's do.** A mention reaches the member it names; an exchange between employees ends at the organization's mention-chain limit; a paused organization, or a budget-paused employee, takes no delivery. The plugin keeps no depth or cursor of its own.
- **The moderator is the first member** until `roadmap.members` names another.
- A `company.db` written by an earlier build may still hold a `roadmap_clones` table and `clone` / `clone_closed` events (the room sessions that build opened). Nothing reads or writes the table any more; the events stay in a roadmap's timeline as recorded. A `roadmaps-relay.json` beside the store is no longer read or written.

## Install

On a Project's Plugins page add `@prismshadow/penguin-plugin-company-roadmaps` (with `@prismshadow/penguin-plugin-company-proposals`), or list both in the Project's `.project_config.toml`:

```toml
plugins = [
  "@prismshadow/penguin-plugin-company-proposals",
  "@prismshadow/penguin-plugin-company-roadmaps",
]
```

## Use

A person opens a roadmap on the **Roadmaps** page, or asks an employee to open one: **Open a roadmap** → a name and one or more of the organization's employees (the first one picked moderates) → **Open**. The page runs the `roadmap.open` Action; the roadmap opens its own unlisted room with those employees and the app goes straight to its room — the channel page, where the moderator's first message is addressed to you, with the draft on the right. Each employee's desk is told which room it is in and how to take part there.

The same request without the page — for a script, or an employee from its session (the opener is then that employee):

```sh
curl -X POST "$API/api/projects/<project>/organizations/<org>/actions/roadmap.open/runs" \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"subject": "organization", "params": {"name": "Queue migration", "employees": ["acme_dev", "acme_web"]}}'
```

## API

`GET /api/company-roadmaps/page` — the page (HTML). `/api/projects/:projectId/organizations/:orgId/roadmaps` — the reads: `GET /[?channel=&status=]` and `GET /:number`. The writes are roadmap Actions on company-proposals' `…/actions/<key>/runs` (`{ subject, params }` → `{ run, result }`): `roadmap.open` (organization; `{ name, employees, channelId?, brief?, parent? }` — without `channelId` the roadmap opens its own unlisted room), `roadmap.draft` (`roadmap:<n>`; `{ record?, body?, items? }`, while discussing; `items` replaces the whole list and the answer's `removed` names the keys it dropped), `roadmap.item.add` (`roadmap:<n>`; one item, as an element of `items`, appended — 409 `item_exists` for a key the draft has) and `roadmap.item.remove { key }` (409 for an item that stands for a proposal or that another is stacked on), both while discussing, `roadmap.establish`, `roadmap.reopen { reason }`, `roadmap.room { channelId }`, `roadmap.rename { name }`, `roadmap.adopt { proposal, title, owner, brief? }`, `roadmap.members { employees, moderator }` (replaces the members and names the moderator; the room's channel follows, and while the room discusses, each new member's desk is told it is there), `roadmap.item.approve` and `roadmap.item.link { proposal }` (`item:<n>/<key>`). Their default guards do not tell a person from an employee, but for `roadmap.members`, which a person or the roadmap's moderator runs; an organization replaces them, and sets the approval roles, with a company workflow (see company-proposals). Every route answers 404 while company mode is off.

## Where things live

The roadmaps are this plugin's tables in the organization's SQLite store, `<root>/<project>/organizations/<org>/company.db` (shared with company-proposals, which writes only its own tables): a roadmap's header with the current record and body, its items, delegations, approvals (each kept with the hash of the brief it was given on) and events; drafts, approvals and events are only ever appended.

## Development

```sh
pnpm --filter @prismshadow/penguin-server build
pnpm --filter @prismshadow/penguin-plugin-company-roadmaps build
pnpm --filter @prismshadow/penguin-plugin-company-roadmaps test
```

`test/integration.test.ts` starts the real server with the plugin installed (`@prismshadow/penguin-plugin-test`); the rest run over fakes of the gateway and company-proposals.
